# Metabolic Reprogramming in Cancer: Mechanisms, Therapeutic Targeting, and Clinical Translation

## Summary
This comprehensive review examines metabolic reprogramming as a fundamental hallmark of cancer, essential for meeting the bioenergetic, biosynthetic, and redox demands of proliferating tumor cells. It explores the foundational mechanisms driving metabolic alterations in glucose, lipid, and amino acid metabolism, heavily influenced by oncogenes like c-MYC, KRAS, and HIF-1α, as well as the inactivation of tumor suppressors such as p53, PTEN, and LKB1. The article details advanced methodological approaches for investigating cancer metabolism, including stable isotope tracing, computational modeling, and single-cell metabolomics. Furthermore, it analyzes the challenges in therapeutic targeting, particularly metabolic plasticity and drug resistance mechanisms, while evaluating emerging strategies for clinical translation. The synthesis highlights how targeting metabolic vulnerabilities through combination therapies, dietary interventions, and immune-metabolic modulation offers promising avenues for precision oncology. Ultimately, understanding the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of tumor metabolism is critical for developing effective, biomarker-driven treatments that overcome adaptive resistance.

## Extended Summary
Metabolic reprogramming is a core hallmark of cancer that enables tumor cells to sustain rapid proliferation, survive in harsh microenvironments, and resist therapeutic interventions. This extensive review delves into the complex metabolic adaptations that characterize malignancies, moving beyond the classical Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis) to encompass dynamic alterations in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, glutaminolysis, lipid synthesis, and nucleotide metabolism. These metabolic shifts are orchestrated by master regulatory networks involving oncogenes such as c-MYC, KRAS, and HIF-1α, alongside the loss of critical tumor suppressors like p53, PTEN, and LKB1. The tumor microenvironment further shapes this metabolic behavior through hypoxia, nutrient competition, and symbiotic interactions with stromal and immune cells, ultimately fostering an immunosuppressive niche.

To dissect these complex metabolic networks, researchers employ advanced analytical technologies, including stable isotope tracing, mass spectrometry-based metabolomics, spatial profiling, and computational modeling. These tools reveal significant intratumoral metabolic heterogeneity and plasticity, which are primary drivers of therapy resistance. Cancer cells dynamically switch between fuel sources, such as glucose, glutamine, and fatty acids, to evade targeted metabolic inhibitors.

Addressing these adaptive resistance mechanisms requires sophisticated therapeutic strategies. The article explores the clinical translation of metabolic inhibitors targeting key enzymes like hexokinase 2, pyruvate kinase M2, lactate dehydrogenase A, and glutaminase. To maximize efficacy and prevent compensatory pathway activation, these agents are increasingly utilized in rational combination therapies alongside conventional chemotherapy, targeted therapies, and immune checkpoint inhibitors. Furthermore, nutritional interventions, such as the ketogenic diet and specific amino acid restrictions, are emerging as metabolic adjuvants that exploit tumor-specific vulnerabilities. The successful clinical implementation of these metabolic therapies relies heavily on the development of robust predictive biomarkers, utilizing advanced imaging and circulating metabolite profiling, to guide patient stratification and optimize personalized oncology treatments.
