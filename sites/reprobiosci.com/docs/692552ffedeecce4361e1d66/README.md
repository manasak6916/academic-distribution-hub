# Single-Cell Sequencing of Ovarian Tissue: From Cellular Atlas to Clinical Translation

## Summary
This comprehensive review details the transformative impact of single-cell sequencing technologies, including scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics, on understanding ovarian biology and pathology. The article synthesizes findings on the cellular composition of the human ovary, creating a detailed atlas of cell types such as oocytes, granulosa, stromal, and immune cells. It highlights how these technologies have resolved long-standing debates, like disproving the existence of oogonial stem cells by reclassifying DDX4-positive cells as perivascular. The review also covers the application of these techniques in mapping developmental trajectories from fetal to adult stages and in studying diseases like Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) and ovarian cancer. It serves as a resource for researchers by providing insights into methodological advancements, experimental protocols, computational workflows, and the identification of novel therapeutic targets and biomarkers, ultimately aiming to advance clinical translation for women's health.

## Extended Summary
This scientific review explores the revolutionary impact of single-cell sequencing on ovarian biology, pathology, and clinical translation. It details how single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatially resolved transcriptomics are creating high-resolution cellular atlases of the ovary, identifying six main somatic cell types (granulosa, stromal, perivascular, endothelial, immune) and oocytes. A key finding highlighted is the resolution of the oogonial stem cell debate, where scRNA-seq analysis demonstrated that DDX4-positive cells are perivascular cells, not germline stem cells, reinforcing the concept of a finite ovarian reserve. The article covers the entire developmental trajectory of the ovary, from fetal germ cell maturation to aging-associated changes in the immune microenvironment. It outlines standardized experimental and computational protocols for tissue dissociation, library preparation, and data analysis using tools like Seurat and CellChat. Furthermore, the review delves into translational applications, particularly in ovarian cancer and Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS). It discusses how high-throughput pharmacotranscriptomic profiling identifies drug resistance mechanisms, such as a CAV1/EGFR feedback loop in response to PI3K/AKT/mTOR inhibitors. Advanced methods like single-cell long-read targeted sequencing for isoform resolution and the integration of scRNA-seq with bulk sequencing for biomarker discovery are also examined, providing a comprehensive guide for researchers and drug developers.
