# Decoding the Genomic Landscape of Premature Ovarian Insufficiency: From Mechanisms to Targeted Therapies

## Summary
Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI) is a significant cause of female infertility, with a strong genetic component accounting for 20-25% of cases. This review synthesizes the latest genomic discoveries in POI, where large-scale whole-exome sequencing (WES) and genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have expanded the known genetic architecture to over 90 genes. The article explores the molecular etiology of POI, covering chromosomal abnormalities, monogenic causes, and complex oligogenic inheritance patterns. Key biological pathways disrupted in POI include meiosis, DNA damage repair, and folliculogenesis. Advanced research methodologies such as Mendelian randomization and single-cell multi-omics are highlighted for their role in identifying novel drug targets, including FANCE and RAB2A. The content addresses the challenges of genetic heterogeneity and the validation of findings through functional studies. It provides a comprehensive resource for researchers and drug development professionals, outlining a translational roadmap for developing targeted diagnostics and interventions to preserve fertility and ovarian function.

## Extended Summary
Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI) is a complex clinical disorder involving the loss of ovarian function before age 40, affecting 1-3.7% of women and causing infertility and long-term health risks. Genetic factors are a major cause, contributing to 20-25% of cases. This review details the expanding genetic landscape of POI, where advanced genomic technologies like whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing have identified over 90 causative genes. The genetic architecture is highly heterogeneous, encompassing chromosomal abnormalities (10-13% of cases), monogenic defects, and increasingly recognized oligogenic and polygenic inheritance patterns. Pathogenic variants disrupt core biological processes essential for ovarian maintenance, with meiosis and DNA repair pathways accounting for nearly half of genetically explained cases, alongside mitochondrial function and folliculogenesis. Advanced analytical methods, such as Mendelian randomization and colocalization analysis, are proving crucial for inferring causality and have identified novel biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets like FANCE and RAB2A, which are involved in DNA repair and autophagy, respectively. The article also discusses the methodological frameworks for gene discovery, including variant interpretation according to ACMG guidelines and the critical need for functional validation using in vitro and in vivo models to confirm pathogenicity. Finally, it explores the translation of these genomic findings into clinical applications, such as comprehensive diagnostic panels, ethical considerations for genetic counseling, and innovative, biomarker-guided clinical trial designs to accelerate the development of personalized therapies for this challenging condition.
