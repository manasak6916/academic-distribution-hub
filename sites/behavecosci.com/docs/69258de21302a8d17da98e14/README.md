## Extended Summary
This comprehensive whitepaper examines the evolutionary mechanisms, research methodologies, and biomedical applications of sexual selection and mating strategies. Originating from Charles Darwin's foundational theories, sexual selection operates through intrasexual competition and intersexual mate choice, driving the evolution of traits that enhance reproductive success, sometimes at the expense of survival. The article delineates the historical controversies surrounding these concepts, including the Darwin-Wallace debate over aesthetic versus utilitarian trait evolution, and addresses complex theoretical frameworks such as the lek paradox, sexual conflict, and mutation-selection balance.

Modern scientific investigations rely on a sophisticated toolkit, including experimental evolution, genomic sequencing, and specialized statistical software like QInfoMating, to quantify deviations from random mating and identify loci under sexual selection. The text explores how these tools are applied across diverse taxa, from Drosophila and guppies to mammals and plants, revealing both universal principles and taxon-specific adaptations. It also investigates the disruptive impact of anthropogenic environmental changes, specifically endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) like 17-beta-Trenbolone, which interfere with the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis and decouple integrated sexual traits.

Beyond fundamental evolutionary biology, the article highlights significant translational applications. In biomedical research, understanding the precise mechanisms of ovulation, cervical mucus regulation, and sperm-egg interaction is driving the discovery of novel non-hormonal contraceptives that align with evolved physiological systems. Additionally, the principles of sexual selection and demo-genetic feedback are crucial for conservation efforts, particularly in designing genetic rescue interventions to prevent extinction vortices in small, isolated populations. Finally, the review synthesizes comparative data on sex differences in longevity, demonstrating how reproductive investments and mating systems shape life history trajectories and aging.
