# Advanced Chemical Methods for Explosives Detection and Analysis: From Foundational Principles to AI-Driven Innovations

## Summary
This article reviews advanced chemical methods for detecting explosives, covering the fundamental chemistry of classes like nitroaromatics, nitramines, and peroxides. Key detection technologies discussed include mass spectrometry (GC-MS, LC-MS), ion mobility spectrometry (IMS), and various spectroscopic methods (Raman, IR, SERS). A significant focus is placed on overcoming challenges such as the extremely low vapor pressure of compounds like RDX and PETN, and mitigating matrix effects in complex environmental or post-blast samples. The review also highlights emerging trends, including fluorescence-based sensors using materials like MOFs and quantum dots, and the transformative impact of integrating chemometrics and machine learning algorithms. These AI-driven approaches enhance data interpretation, improve threat classification accuracy, and reduce false alarm rates, paving the way for more robust and field-deployable detection systems.

## Extended Summary
This comprehensive review details the state-of-the-art in the chemical detection and analysis of explosives. It begins by categorizing explosive materials into primary chemical classes, including nitroaromatics (TNT), nitramines (RDX, HMX), nitrate esters (PETN), and peroxides (TATP), outlining their unique properties that inform detection strategies. The article provides an in-depth examination of core analytical technologies. These include separation techniques like Gas and Liquid Chromatography coupled with Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS, LC-MS), which are considered gold standards for identification. Ion Mobility Spectrometry (IMS) is highlighted for its widespread use in field screening due to its speed and portability. Spectroscopic methods, such as Raman, Surface-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS), and Infrared (IR) spectroscopy, are discussed for their ability to provide specific molecular fingerprints. A major focus is on innovative fluorescence-based sensing platforms, which utilize materials like Metal-Organic Frameworks (MOFs) and quantum dots that exhibit fluorescence quenching in the presence of explosive molecules. The review thoroughly addresses critical real-world challenges, such as detecting low-volatility compounds like RDX and PETN, analyzing complex post-blast residues, and mitigating matrix effects that can compromise analytical accuracy. Finally, it emphasizes the transformative role of chemometrics and artificial intelligence, detailing how machine learning algorithms are being integrated to process complex data, enhance pattern recognition, and improve the accuracy and speed of threat classification, which is crucial for both forensic admissibility and field deployment.
